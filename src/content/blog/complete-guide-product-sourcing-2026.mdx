Product sourcing has never been more complicated — or more full of opportunity. Tariffs have reshuffled the cost math between regions. Nearshoring to Mexico has moved from fringe strategy to mainstream consideration. And buyers who used to accept whatever their distributor charged are discovering they can access factory-direct pricing on products they buy every year.

This guide is the hub. It covers every major sourcing region and channel, gives you a decision framework for choosing your strategy, and links to the deeper guides where you need them.

## The Major Sourcing Regions and What They're Good For

No single region dominates all product categories. The right choice depends on what you're making, in what quantities, with what quality requirements, and what your tariff exposure looks like.

### China

Still the world's most capable and cost-competitive manufacturing base for most product categories. China's strengths:

- **Depth of the supplier ecosystem.** For virtually any product category, there are hundreds of Chinese factories competing on price and capability. The competition keeps prices honest and gives buyers genuine options.
- **Integrated supply chains.** Raw materials, components, tooling, and finished goods are often within a single industrial cluster. A plastics factory in Dongguan is minutes from the injection mold shop, the resin supplier, and the logistics hub.
- **Flexibility on quantities.** Many Chinese factories will quote 500-unit runs that US manufacturers won't touch.

China's weaknesses in 2026:

- **Tariffs are real.** Section 301, IEEPA, and category-specific tariffs have added 30-50% to the landed cost of many Chinese goods. [Look up your HTS code](https://lgistics.ai) before assuming China is cheapest.
- **Lead times.** Factory-to-warehouse in the US is 14-20 weeks for ocean freight.
- **Quality control requires work.** Sampling, pre-shipment inspection, and supplier verification are non-negotiable for new factory relationships.

For the full China sourcing process, read our [complete China sourcing guide](/blog/source-products-from-china-2026). For what Alibaba doesn't tell you, read [this](/blog/alibaba-sourcing-what-they-dont-tell-you).

### United States

US manufacturing is more competitive in 2026 than it was five years ago — tariffs have narrowed the cost gap considerably for many categories, and lead time advantages are real. US strengths:

- No import duties, shorter supply chains, easier quality oversight
- "Made in USA" as a legitimate differentiator for some buyer segments
- Advantages for heavy/bulky products where ocean freight costs eat into China's labor savings
- Realistic option for smaller order quantities where ocean freight economics don't work

US weaknesses: meaningfully higher unit cost on labor-intensive products, smaller pool of manufacturers for some categories, and less manufacturing depth in consumer goods.

For a full US vs China cost comparison with real numbers, see our [cost comparison article](/blog/us-vs-china-manufacturing-cost-comparison). For how to find US manufacturers, see our [US manufacturer sourcing guide](/blog/how-to-find-us-manufacturers).

### Mexico

Mexico has emerged as the most significant nearshoring beneficiary from US-China trade tensions. Key advantages:

- **No Section 301 or IEEPA tariffs** (USMCA applies, with most goods at 0%)
- **2-5 day ground freight to US** vs 4-6 weeks by sea from China
- **Lower labor cost than the US** (though higher than China for most categories)
- **Proximity for quality oversight** — you can visit a Monterrey factory in a half-day trip from most US cities

Mexico has manufacturing depth in automotive components, electronics assembly, aerospace parts, appliances, and food and beverage. Consumer goods and general merchandise are less developed. Lead times to establish a Mexican supplier relationship are typically 6-18 months.

### Vietnam

Vietnam absorbed substantial manufacturing investment after the initial round of US-China tariffs in 2018-2019. Vietnamese manufacturing strengths:

- Apparel, footwear, and textiles — Vietnam is now the #2 apparel exporter to the US after China
- Electronics assembly — major consumer electronics brands have shifted production here
- Furniture and home goods
- Labor cost roughly comparable to or slightly above China for many product types

Weaknesses: raw material supply chains are still largely China-dependent for many categories (so "Made in Vietnam" goods often still have significant Chinese content), factory capacity at scale is more limited than China, and English language capability varies.

### India

India is a compelling sourcing option for specific categories, less so for general manufacturing:

- **Pharmaceuticals and chemicals** — India is the world's pharmacy, with deep API manufacturing capability
- **Textiles and apparel** — India competes with China and Vietnam for many fabric and garment categories
- **IT and engineering services** — not physical manufacturing, but relevant for product development
- **Machined parts and metal fabrication** — emerging capability, especially in Pune and Chennai

India's challenges: logistics infrastructure is improving but still creates friction, manufacturing cluster depth doesn't match China for most consumer product categories, and lead times can be longer than China due to port and customs complexity.

## The Decision Framework

Here's how we think about sourcing region selection for most products:

**Step 1: Calculate your China tariff exposure.** [Look up your HTS code](https://lgistics.ai) and calculate total combined tariff rate (MFN + Section 301 + IEEPA). If combined tariffs exceed 25%, run a full landed cost comparison before defaulting to China.

**Step 2: Assess product characteristics.** Is your product labor-intensive (favors China/Vietnam/India) or capital-intensive (closer to cost parity)? Is it heavy relative to its value (higher freight cost per dollar, favors domestic or nearshore)? Does it require specialized tooling or equipment (look for clusters with that capability)?

**Step 3: Evaluate your quantity and frequency.** Small, infrequent orders favor US or domestic options — the logistics overhead of international sourcing is high relative to the savings at small scale. High-volume, recurring orders justify the setup cost of an overseas supplier relationship.

**Step 4: Consider your lead time tolerance.** If your business model requires 2-3 week replenishment cycles, US or Mexico are your options. If you can forecast 4-5 months out and carry appropriate safety stock, ocean freight from Asia works fine.

**Step 5: Factor in total cost, not unit cost.** Unit cost is the starting point, not the answer. [Landed cost calculation](/blog/landed-cost-calculator-importing-china) — including freight, duties, insurance, and customs — is what actually determines profitability.

## Sourcing Channels: Which One to Use

Beyond region, you need to decide how you engage with suppliers:

**Direct from factory** is the goal for most high-volume buyers. You get the best pricing, the most control, and the deepest relationship. You also take on all the discovery, verification, and management work yourself.

**Trading companies** add cost (15-40% in many cases) but reduce complexity. For buyers who need multiple product types from a single region, or who don't have the bandwidth to manage factory relationships directly, a reputable trading company can be worth the premium.

**Distributors** are appropriate for commodity products where the convenience of local stock and standard payment terms is worth the markup. For ongoing, high-volume purchases of definable products, distributor pricing is usually 40-60% over factory cost — significant savings available if you have the volume to justify direct sourcing.

**Managed sourcing services** handle the sourcing process end-to-end: finding suppliers, managing quality, coordinating logistics. Unlike traditional sourcing agents, modern managed sourcing platforms give you transparency into pricing and supplier selection while offloading the operational burden.

## What Makes a Sourcing Strategy Sustainable

We've worked with buyers who save 30% in year one and then slowly drift back to their original suppliers because the ongoing management burden is too high. The best sourcing strategies are ones you can actually maintain.

**Document everything.** Specs, supplier contacts, quote history, quality standards, inspection results. When a supplier relationship goes sideways or a key employee leaves, your documentation is what lets you recover quickly.

**Don't single-source.** For any critical product, maintain at least one qualified backup supplier. This is especially true for China-sourced goods where geopolitical or logistics disruptions are a genuine risk.

**Review costs annually.** Markets change, tariff rates change, supplier capabilities change. A sourcing decision that was optimal in 2023 may not be optimal in 2026.

**Understand your categories.** The more you understand about how your product is made — what materials, what processes, what certifications — the better you can evaluate supplier claims, catch substitutions, and negotiate intelligently.
