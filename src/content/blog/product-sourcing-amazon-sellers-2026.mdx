Sourcing for an Amazon business is a different discipline than sourcing in general, because your cost structure has two extra layers most importers don't carry — Amazon's fees and the brutal price transparency of a marketplace where your competitor's listing is one click away. A product that would be profitable in a normal wholesale business can lose money on Amazon once FBA fees, PPC, and returns come out. This playbook is about sourcing in a way that survives that math.

## Start From the Margin, Not the Product

Most new sellers fall in love with a product, source it, then discover the unit economics don't work. Reverse it. Before you source anything, model the full cost stack against the price the market will actually bear:

- **Landed cost** — FOB + freight + duty + fees, per unit ([full breakdown here](/blog/how-to-calculate-landed-cost-from-china))
- **Amazon referral fee** — typically ~15% of sale price
- **FBA fulfillment fee** — size/weight-based, per unit
- **Storage** — especially the penalty for slow-movers
- **PPC / advertising** — often the largest variable cost for a new launch
- **Returns and reimbursements** — category-dependent, real

Only after all of that comes your profit. The number that matters is landed cost as a fraction of sale price: as a rule of thumb, if your landed cost is more than ~25–35% of your selling price, the FBA and advertising layers will squeeze your margin to nothing. Source to hit that ratio, or don't source it.

## Tariffs Changed the Math — Know Your Current Number

For Amazon sellers importing from China, tariffs are a first-order variable, not a footnote — they hit the landed-cost line directly and can be the difference between a viable product and a dead one. Rates have also been volatile and legally contested through 2025–2026, so a number from an old guide is dangerous. Look up your specific product's current combined rate before you commit — [here's how the tariff layers work](/blog/china-tariffs-2026-importers-guide) — and if your category carries a high duty, seriously price [alternatives to China](/blog/sourcing-vietnam-india-mexico-china-alternatives) and even [US manufacturing](/blog/us-vs-china-manufacturing-cost-comparison) before assuming China wins.

## MOQ Strategy: Test Cheap, Scale on Winners

The Amazon private-label game is a portfolio game — most products underperform, a few carry the business — so your sourcing strategy should be built to test cheaply and scale only the winners. That means fighting for low first-order quantities even at a higher unit price:

- Order the smallest viable first run to validate demand, accepting a worse per-unit price as the cost of the test — [how to negotiate that MOQ down](/blog/moq-negotiation-lower-minimums).
- Use existing molds and stock options to avoid custom tooling on an unproven product.
- Reserve the aggressive price negotiation and volume commitments for products that have proven they sell.

Spending $8,000 on a 10,000-unit first order of a product that flops is how new sellers go broke. Spending $1,500 to test 2,000 units, then reordering big on the one that works, is how they survive.

## Vet Hard — Your Account Depends on It

For an Amazon seller, a bad supplier isn't just a bad order — it's a quality problem that becomes 1-star reviews, and reviews are the asset your whole listing rests on. A defective run can tank a product permanently. So the [factory audit and verification](/blog/factory-audit-checklist-first-order) matters more here than almost anywhere: verify the supplier, hold a tight [spec](/blog/how-to-write-product-spec-manufacturing), and never skip the pre-shipment inspection tied to your final payment. Consistency across reorders is what protects your review velocity.

## The Sourcing Mistakes That Kill Amazon Businesses

- **Pricing on FOB, not landed cost** — the classic, fatal error; your real cost is 40–100% higher than the quote.
- **Ignoring the current tariff** — a rate change can flip a winner to a loser overnight.
- **Over-ordering an unproven product** — tying up cash and warehouse space in inventory that won't sell.
- **Single-sourcing a hero product** — one supplier failure and your best listing goes out of stock; keep a qualified backup, and [know how to switch](/blog/switch-suppliers-without-disruption) without disruption.
- **Chasing the lowest unit price into a quality problem** — the [red flags in a cheap quote](/blog/how-to-read-supplier-quote-red-flags) are usually the material or the weight, and they show up in your reviews.

## The Bottom Line

Source Amazon products backward from the margin: model landed cost plus FBA fees plus PPC against the real market price before you commit, and only source what clears the ratio. Test new products at low volume even at a premium, scale hard on proven winners, vet suppliers like your reviews depend on it — because they do — and keep your current tariff number in front of you at all times.
