Alibaba is the storefront China shows the world. 1688 (1688.com, run by the same parent, Alibaba Group) is where Chinese businesses actually buy from each other. Same factories in many cases — but priced for the domestic market, without the export markup, the English-speaking sales team, or the trading companies that pad Alibaba listings. For the same item, 1688 prices commonly run 20–40% below the Alibaba quote.

That gap is real, and it's why experienced importers use 1688. But it comes with friction: the entire platform is in Chinese, most suppliers won't ship internationally or take foreign payment, and the buyer protections you lean on with Alibaba are thinner. Here's how to use it anyway, what the workarounds cost, and when the price difference is worth the extra steps.

## What 1688 Is (and Isn't)

1688 is a domestic wholesale marketplace. Its sellers assume their buyer is a Chinese company with a Chinese bank account, a mainland shipping address, and the ability to communicate in Chinese. That single assumption explains almost every difference from Alibaba:

- **Prices are lower** because listings target domestic resellers, not exporters, and skip the trading-company layer that marks up a large share of Alibaba listings.
- **MOQs are often lower** and more flexible, because domestic wholesale runs on smaller, frequent orders rather than container-load exports.
- **Payment is domestic** — Alipay and Chinese bank transfer, not the international payment rails and escrow (Trade Assurance) that Alibaba supports.
- **Shipping is domestic** — most sellers ship within China only. Getting goods out of the country is your problem.
- **There's no export hand-holding.** No English, no export documentation, no help with customs or compliance paperwork.

The price is genuinely better. The overhead of bridging those gaps is the cost of capturing it — and whether that trade makes sense depends entirely on your volume.

## The Two Ways Foreign Buyers Use 1688

**Option 1: A sourcing agent.** The standard approach, and the right one for most people starting out. An agent (an individual or a service) acts as your Chinese buyer: they communicate with suppliers in Chinese, place and pay for orders domestically, consolidate goods at their warehouse, inspect, and arrange export shipping to you. You pay the 1688 price plus the agent's fee — commonly **3–10% of order value**, or a flat per-order fee for small orders. A good agent pays for itself: if 1688 is 30% cheaper than Alibaba and the agent takes 8%, you're still 22% ahead, and you've offloaded the language, payment, and consolidation work.

**Option 2: Direct, with a domestic bridge.** If you have a Chinese entity, a partner on the ground, or a freight forwarder who'll accept goods at a mainland address and handle export, you can buy more directly and keep the agent's margin. This captures the most value but requires infrastructure most importers don't have when they start. It becomes worth building once your China volume is large enough that the agent fees exceed the cost of your own setup.

Either way, you'll navigate a Chinese-language site. Browser translation handles the listings well enough to shop; the negotiation, order details, and dispute handling are where an agent or a Chinese-speaking partner earns their keep.

### How to Choose and Vet an Agent

Not all agents are equal, and the wrong one erases your savings or worse. Look for: transparent fee structure (a clear percentage or flat fee, not a hidden markup on the goods), willingness to show you the original 1688 listing and price, inspection capability, and references or a track record. The red flag is an agent who won't show you the underlying price — that's how a "3% agent" quietly becomes a 25% markup. A trustworthy agent's margin is the fee, not a spread on the goods.

## How to Actually Search 1688

Keyword search in English mostly fails — you need Chinese search terms. Two reliable methods:

**Image search — the power move.** 1688's image search is excellent. Upload a photo of the product, or a screenshot of an Alibaba listing, and it surfaces the domestic-market equivalents — frequently including the *actual factory* behind the Alibaba reseller you were about to pay a markup to. This is the fastest way to find the real source and the real price, and it sidesteps the language barrier entirely because you're searching by picture. The workflow: find the item on Alibaba, screenshot it, run the image through 1688, and compare what the same product costs domestically.

**Translated keywords.** Translate your product term into Chinese and search that. Get the translation right — the wrong characters return the wrong category, and industry terms often don't translate literally. When in doubt, image search beats keyword search on 1688 every time.

The image-search route matters for a reason beyond convenience: it lets you find a product on Alibaba, then trace it back to its 1688 listing to see what the domestic price actually is. Even if you ultimately buy through Alibaba for the export support and buyer protection, that domestic number is priceless negotiating leverage. When you tell an Alibaba supplier you know the item sells for a certain price on 1688, the conversation changes fast.

## A Worked Cost Comparison

Say you're sourcing a small consumer good. The Alibaba quote is $1.00/unit for 10,000 units — $10,000. The same item traces to a 1688 listing at $0.68/unit. Through an agent charging 8%:

- 1688 goods: 10,000 × $0.68 = $6,800
- Agent fee (8%): $544
- Domestic consolidation + a bit of handling: call it $150
- **Subtotal before international freight: ~$7,494**

You still pay international freight and duties either way, so those wash out of the comparison. On the goods themselves, you're at roughly $7,494 versus $10,000 — about 25% saved after the agent's cut, on a single order. At recurring volume, that difference funds a lot: on $100,000/year of purchasing, a 25% cut is $25,000 back to your margin.

The catch: this only pencils out when the absolute dollars are big enough to matter. On a 500-unit first order, saving 25% of a small number isn't worth the added steps and thinner protection.

## Risks and How to Manage Them

1688 shifts risk onto you or your agent. The main ones:

- **Thinner buyer protection.** No Trade Assurance escrow like Alibaba. Mitigate with an agent who inspects before paying the supplier, and by [verifying the supplier](/blog/factory-audit-checklist-first-order) as you would anywhere.
- **Quality variance.** Domestic listings are aimed at buyers who can walk into the factory. Insist on samples and an inspection through your agent.
- **Export and compliance gaps.** The supplier won't handle export docs or certifications. Your agent or forwarder covers export; you handle compliance for your market.
- **Language-driven misunderstandings.** Specs and terms get lost in translation. A tight written [product spec](/blog/how-to-write-product-spec-manufacturing) and a competent agent close most of that gap.

## When 1688 Is Worth It — and When It Isn't

**Worth it when:** you're sourcing commodity or lightly customized goods, your volume is enough that a 20–40% price cut is real money, and you either have an agent or are willing to get one. Packaging, hardware, textiles, small consumer goods — 1688's sweet spots.

**Not worth it when:** you need heavy customization, tight compliance documentation (FDA, CE, UL) with export paperwork, or supplier accountability you can enforce — areas where Alibaba's export-oriented sellers and Trade Assurance are genuinely better. It's also rarely worth the friction on a small first order where the absolute savings are modest and the priority is de-risking, not squeezing cost.

The honest trade: 1688 gives you the domestic price but shifts the export, payment, and quality-control burden onto you or your agent. ## Quick FAQ

**Do I need to speak Chinese?** To browse, no — image search and translation are enough. To buy and negotiate reliably, you need an agent or a Chinese-speaking partner.

**Is 1688 cheaper than Alibaba for everything?** Usually cheaper on commodity and lightly-customized goods, but the gap narrows on complex or highly-certified products where Alibaba's export sellers add real value.

**Can I pay a 1688 supplier directly with a US card?** Generally no — they expect domestic payment (Alipay/Chinese bank). That's the main reason foreign buyers use agents.

## The Practical Takeaway

Use 1688's image search on anything you're already sourcing on Alibaba — that alone is worth the effort as pure intelligence, revealing the real factory and the domestic price. To actually buy, get a reputable sourcing agent unless you have a Chinese entity or partner, and expect to pay 3–10% for it. And be clear-eyed about the trade: you're exchanging convenience and buyer protection for a materially lower price. For high-volume commodity goods, that trade is usually right.
